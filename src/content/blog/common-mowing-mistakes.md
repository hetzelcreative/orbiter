Mowing seems simple enough — just cut the grass, right? But the way you mow has a bigger impact on your lawn's health than most people realize. Here are four common mistakes we see all the time.

## 1. Cutting Too Short

This is the number one mistake. Scalping your lawn stresses the grass, exposes the soil to sunlight (which helps weeds), and weakens the root system. We recommend keeping your lawn at 3–3.5 inches for cool-season grasses in Iowa.

## 2. Mowing on a Fixed Schedule Instead of by Height

Don't mow every Saturday just because it's Saturday. Mow when the grass needs it — generally when it's about one-third taller than your target height. In peak growing season, that might mean every 5–6 days. In summer heat, it might be every 10.

## 3. Using Dull Blades

Dull mower blades tear the grass instead of cutting it cleanly. This leaves ragged, brown-tipped edges and makes your lawn more susceptible to disease. Sharpen your blades at least once or twice per season.

## 4. Mowing Wet Grass

We get it — sometimes you just need to get it done. But mowing wet grass leads to uneven cuts, clumping, and can spread fungal diseases. If you can wait for the lawn to dry, your turf will thank you.
