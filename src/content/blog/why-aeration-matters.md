If there's one service we wish more homeowners took advantage of, it's core aeration. It's not flashy, and you won't see instant results — but the long-term payoff for your lawn is massive.

## What Is Aeration?

Core aeration uses a machine to pull small plugs of soil out of your lawn. These plugs break down naturally over a week or two, and the holes left behind allow air, water, and nutrients to penetrate deeper into the root zone.

## Why Does It Matter?

Over time, the soil beneath your lawn becomes compacted — especially in high-traffic areas and clay-heavy soil like we have in Southwest Iowa. Compacted soil makes it harder for roots to grow, water to absorb, and fertilizer to reach where it's needed.

Aeration solves all of that.

## When Should You Aerate?

Fall is the gold standard for aeration in Iowa — typically September through mid-October. The cooler temperatures and fall rains create ideal conditions for your lawn to recover and fill in.

## Pair It with Overseeding

Aeration and overseeding are a power combo. The holes from aeration give new seed perfect soil contact, dramatically improving germination rates. If your lawn has thin spots, this one-two punch is the fastest way to a thicker, healthier yard.
