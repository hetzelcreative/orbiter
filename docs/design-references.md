# Design References — Home-Services Sites

A working design brief for the **orbiter** starter. Below are concrete design and layout patterns pulled from a curated set of home-services websites (roofing, HVAC, pest, fencing, plumbing, drain, construction, demolition, rentals). Use it as a source of reusable hero, section, color, type, and trust-signal conventions when designing a new local-business site. Notes were captured via automated page analysis, so treat specific color/font calls as directional rather than pixel-exact.

## Design principles for orbiter home-services sites

Synthesis of the recurring winning patterns across all 25 sites. These are the defaults orbiter should make easy; deviations should be deliberate.

### Recurring homepage section sequence
A near-universal skeleton emerged. Orbiter's homepage should default to roughly this order:
1. **Hero** — image/video with overlay headline + dual CTA + inline trust signals
2. **Trust badges / credential row** — certifications, ratings, years-in-business icons (often immediately under hero)
3. **Service grid** — 3–8 icon/image cards, one per core service (this is the primary nav-into-detail surface)
4. **Why-choose-us / value props** — 3–9 benefit blocks or pillars
5. **Social proof** — review aggregation and/or testimonials carousel
6. **Project gallery / before-after** (visual trades: roofing, construction, fencing, demolition)
7. **Process steps** — 3–5 numbered "how it works" row (build/reno/inspection verticals)
8. **Financing / promotions / coupons** block (HVAC & plumbing especially)
9. **Service-area list or map** — supports local SEO and signals coverage
10. **Blog/tips preview** (when active), then **contact form** and **footer** with full NAP.

Not every site uses every block, but the top five (hero → badges → services → why-us → reviews) are effectively mandatory.

### Hero conventions
- **Imagery:** Real photography of work/crew/trucks is most common; video (All County) and vehicle/van or mascot illustrations (Vector, Maston's, Hall's, APE, Economy) are effective differentiators. Text-first heroes (Buehler, Pinnacle, Perfect Timing, C&M) work for technical/credential-led positioning.
- **Headline pattern:** Either (a) location + service ("Orange County's Trusted... Roofing Contractor," "Roofing Contractor in Omaha, NE"), (b) longevity ("...Since 1971/1989/1945"), or (c) short benefit/personality line ("Get It Done Right. Get It Done Safely," "No Monkey Business"). Bake the city/service into the headline for SEO.
- **Dual CTA is the standard:** a scheduling/quote button PLUS a click-to-call phone. Text-to-schedule (x3worx) and instant-quote (Hall's) are strong secondary options.
- **Inline hero trust signals:** star rating + review count, years in business, licensing/bonded/insured, or a marquee certification — placed in or immediately below the hero, not buried.

### How services/categories are presented
- **Card grid is the dominant pattern:** each service = icon (or photo) + name + one-line blurb + link. 3, 4, 6, or 8 cards depending on catalog size.
- A **consistent icon system** (snowflake/radiator/wrench; category illustrations) reads as intentional and aids scanning — orbiter should ship a coherent icon set, not mixed styles.
- Larger catalogs use **mega-menus / tabbed or filtered carousels**; keep these organized by category to avoid the overload seen on the sprawling-menu sites.
- Multi-market businesses benefit from **location-selector buttons or location cards** (Blackburn) — pairs directly with orbiter's programmatic `[slug]` location pages.

### Trust / social-proof placement
- **Two tiers:** (1) a compact credential/badge row high up (BBB, manufacturer certs like GAF Master Elite / Carrier Authorized, Angi, years-in-business), and (2) a dedicated reviews/testimonials section lower down.
- **Volume sells:** clustering many review-platform logos (Google, Yelp, BBB, HomeAdvisor, Facebook) and showing review counts ("350+", "1,000+ Reviews", "300+ Google Reviews") builds credibility — feeds directly into orbiter's DataForSEO review pipeline.
- **Humanizing signals** recur: team photos, named technicians in testimonials, staff spotlights, family-owned / woman-owned / locally-owned badges.
- **Numeric stat callouts** (34+ years, 10K+ roofs, 2500+ customers) in large weight are a cheap, high-impact trust device.

### CTA & phone-number prominence
- Phone number appears in the **top bar, hero, and footer** at minimum; the best sites repeat the same CTA 4–6 times down the page (Pinnacle, Maston's) without feeling spammy.
- **Sticky/persistent CTA** (header "Book Now"/"Schedule Service" and often a mobile sticky call bar) is near-universal.
- Emergency/24-7 and same-day messaging is a common urgency lever for HVAC/plumbing/drain.
- Forms follow the orbiter Netlify pattern; **multi-step progressive forms** (Genuine Pest) lift completion and are worth offering as a variant.

### Color tendencies
- **Muted, professional base dominates:** navy / dark gray / charcoal + white, letting photography carry visual energy. This is the safe, high-trust default.
- **One warm accent** — orange is by far the most common (Pacific, All County, APE, JD Ferro, Maston's, Buehler, Hall's, Perfect Timing) — reserved for CTAs and highlights. Teal/green (Genuine, Macawsome) and pink (Chase) are effective brand-differentiator accents.
- Reinforces orbiter's token discipline: a neutral primary + a single saturated accent scale driven entirely from `@theme`. Avoid hardcoded one-off colors.

### Typography tendencies
- **Modern sans-serif everywhere.** Headings trend **bold and often condensed/uppercase** for punch and scanability; body stays generous and legible.
- Clear hierarchy (large display value props, mid-weight subheads, readable body) matters more than typeface novelty.
- Distinctive **brand voice in copy** (Buehler's playful tone, APE/Hall's mascot puns) is what separates memorable sites from templated ones — the type system stays disciplined while the words carry personality.

### Imagery style
- Authentic local photography (crews, trucks, completed jobs) over stock; **before/after and project galleries** are essential for visual trades.
- **Mascots and van/vehicle illustrations** are a recurring, low-cost differentiator that adds personality without breaking the professional palette.
- Manufacturer/brand-partner logo carousels reinforce quality by association.

### Mobile considerations
- Hamburger menu with nested/expandable service categories is standard; keep taxonomy shallow to avoid mega-menu overload on small screens.
- Persistent header phone/booking button and (ideally) a sticky bottom call bar.
- Card grids reflow to single-column; large tap targets on CTAs; trust signals stay above the fold on mobile too.

### What reads professional/high-converting vs. templated
- **Professional/high-converting:** disciplined muted palette + one accent; consistent icon system; trust signals layered high and repeated; dual CTA with click-to-call; authentic photography; numeric proof; a genuine brand voice/mascot; clean shallow navigation; location-aware content.
- **Templated/weak:** hero with no inline trust signal; generic stock imagery; inconsistent or mismatched icons; a single buried CTA; sprawling unstructured mega-menus (cognitive overload); off-palette one-off colors and gradients; and no local/service-area specificity. Orbiter should make the professional set the path of least resistance and treat the weak set as anti-patterns.
